# 8. AUTHOR'S PERSPECTIVE

## Author's Perspective: Toward a New Architecture of Peace in the Age of Artificial Intelligence

*Dr. Fatih Sayin*  
Editor-in-Chief, FMS Strategic Review  
FMS Expert Agents Think Tank

---

When we founded the FMS Expert Agents Think Tank, we posed a question that sounds technical but is profoundly human: whether artificial intelligence will compress the path to war or widen the path to durable peace. After directing thirteen domain-specialized agents through structured deliberation—thirty-one debate turns, four cross-agent challenges, and a consensus merge scoring 0.60 confidence—I offer this perspective not as a technologist's boast but as a peace researcher's caution.

**We are not forecasting inevitability. We are describing a choice.**

### The Historical Moment

Every major communication technology has reshaped diplomacy. The telegraph accelerated mobilization in the nineteenth century; the Cold War hotline and satellite verification built trust when political will existed (Headrick, 2012; United Nations, 2018). Artificial intelligence differs in three respects: **speed** (models update weekly; treaties take years), **opacity** (black-box recommendations reach foreign ministers), and **scale** (synthetic media can fabricate casus belli before debunking) (Brundage et al., 2018; Chesney & Citron, 2019).

The norm-building window identified in this volume may close within this decade. I have seen foreign ministries procure diplomatic AI faster than parliaments can oversee it—a pattern that repeats across OECD readiness reports (OECD, 2024). Without shared semantics for crisis alerts, we risk **war by misunderstanding** (RAND Corporation, 2020; Geist & Lohn, 2022).

### Why a Peace Architecture, Not Only a Compact

The Global Diplomatic AI Compact (GDAIC) is necessary but not sufficient. Compacts govern tools; **architectures govern systems**. The FMS Peace Architecture Framework™ places GDAIC within seven pillars because peace is never achieved by technology alone. Preventive diplomacy fails without economic justice. AI governance fails without human dignity. Multilateral cooperation fails without educating the diplomats of 2040 today.

I draw intellectually from Galtung's distinction between negative and positive peace, Richmond's critique of liberal peace templates, and the UN Secretary-General's *New Agenda for Peace* (Galtung, 1969; Richmond, 2016; United Nations, 2023). The framework responds to Richmond and Autesserre: we do not export algorithmic settlement templates. We confine AI to analytic and communicative support, with local validation and inclusive design (Autesserre, 2014).

### Evidence That Grounds Vision

Vision without evidence is propaganda. This volume grounds claims in:

- **59 state-based armed conflicts** in 2023 (UCDP)—the post-1945 record (Uppsala University, 2024).  
- **>120 million** forcibly displaced persons (UNHCR, 2024).  
- **$2.44 trillion** global military expenditure versus prevention pilots priced at **$97–158M** short-term (SIPRI, 2024).  
- UN High-Level Advisory Body consensus that AI governance must be **inclusive and rights-based** (United Nations, 2024).

These numbers do not prove AI will save peace. They prove that **underinvestment in prevention is a policy failure**, not a resource constraint.

### What I Believe Is Possible

By 2030, I believe we can achieve: a UN Envoy on AI and International Peace; GDAIC consultation with 60–80 adherents under the moderate scenario; provenance standards for P5 hotlines; and three evaluated regional pilots demonstrating measurable humanitarian access improvements. By 2050—developed fully in Chapter 18—I envision diplomatic AI regulated like civil aviation: inspected, shared, trusted.

I do not believe war will be eliminated. Human conflict persists because injustice persists. But the **threshold for interstate war** can rise if early, dignified, inclusive diplomatic intervention becomes cheaper, faster, and more legitimate than force (United Nations, 2023).

### What Keeps Me Awake

Three risks dominate my concern:

1. **Synthetic casus belli**—a deepfake of a nuclear-armed leader during crisis (5–8% illustrative black swan probability).  
2. **Algorithmic bloc rivalry**—incompatible crisis dashboards between major powers (20–25% worst-case probability).  
3. **Humanitarian data weaponization**—beneficiary databases fused with targeting intelligence.

Each is preventable through provenance, firewalls, and political choice **before** crisis, not after catastrophe.

### A Personal Commitment

As Editor-in-Chief, I commit FMS Strategic Review to:

- Annual empirical evaluation of pilots—not vendor marketing.  
- Live multi-agent sessions in Volume 1, Issue 2 with country-specific annexes.  
- Transparent citation verification (Appendix: Citation Verification Log).  
- Centering conflict-affected voices in framework updates.

The mission endures: **Building Peace Through Intelligence, Diplomacy, and Human Dignity.** Technology amplifies values already held—or already violated. Our task is to ensure diplomatic AI amplifies the best of multilateralism.
